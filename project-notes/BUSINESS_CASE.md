# ShiftSwap Business Case Report

## 1. The Idea
At the hockey shop, missing a shift—especially on the skate sharpening counter—created a huge domino effect. We'd get backed up with skates the next day because there were so many left to grind, which meant we couldn't sharpen other customers' skates even when they really needed them done right away. I built ShiftShop so everyone knows where they're supposed to be working and swapping shifts with coworkers is way easier. A manager posts all the shifts for the week, month, or whatever timeframe they use; staff sign up for them, and if someone can't make a shift, they drop it, and a notification pops up for everyone else so someone can pick it up. Pricing is $10/month for limited access and $40/month for unlimited access.

The worst moment was when we couldn't give new skates out because nobody was sharpening, which just caused a huge backup. A lot of the time it's really just people leaving places unattended to help out in a different station.

Working on the floor of a busy hockey shop made those gaps stand out fast. Schedules get pulled around by weekend rushes, minor hockey tournaments, school classes, and cold snaps that bring skaters in right before ice time. When someone got sick or had a conflict come up, getting covered usually meant texting coworkers one by one or leaving a note near the back counter. On a quiet weekday a late swap barely mattered, but on a busy Saturday afternoon before a game, an open spot at the counter backs up the whole store.

Skate sharpening takes real hands-on skill, so you can't just pull any worker off the floor to run the machine. Someone has to mount the skates right, set the hollow, check the edges, and keep things moving without messing up expensive skates. ShiftSwap—which I first called ShiftShop—puts shift posting, claiming, and swaps in one spot on a phone or laptop, with a $10/month limited tier and a $40/month unlimited tier.

## 2. Where IT Creates Value
When people are not sure where they are supposed to be or you're short-staffed, it creates longer lines, especially in the skates section, and just slows down all operations within the store.

Fitting gets very backed up, as there is only a limited amount of room for people to get skates fit. There were times when managers would go looking for people so that they could direct them around, and this made it so that they couldn't help the customers during busy times.

The line was stuck at the skate fitting section, as everyone had to get their skates fitted to their feet. As well, people would be standing around the front, walking around, and waiting for their skates to be done, causing the store to be full. The first thing to pile up was always either skate fitting or sharpening, as those were the most time-consuming activities and the most popular as well. It caused them to not greet and ask the customers if they needed help. Managers are the most knowledgeable people on the floor, and teams need them on the floor helping customers, not employees.

Putting schedule info and swaps on one web page gives staff and managers clear answers right away instead of digging through old texts or guessing who covered a shift. Cleaner records of who worked and where gaps happened also help managers plan future schedules better and cut down on wasted time during busy rushes.

## 3. Network Effects
The more staff that check the app weekly, the quicker shifts will be taken, and if they are taken, this also shows on our fill-rate simulator.

When more people on the team use the app, it allows the workplace to flow better; people can get the shifts that they want, and others can stick to already made plans with friends or family. Well, when usage goes up, it allows for more shifts to be filled when last-minute changes need to be added.

The fill rate simulator shows the accurate results, and shifts get filled much faster the more that people are on. If only two or three people check now and then, dropped shifts sit open and force people back to texting one by one. When more staff check weekly—or whenever they want extra hours or need to move a shift—open spots get picked up faster, and coworkers looking for hours can grab them without awkward group-chat messages.

Starting at one busy shop of around 20 to 30 staff helps build real habits around using the board instead of side texts before expanding to nearby stores.

## 4. Can It Be Copied?
People can make a basic site pretty fast now, especially with AI, so just having the idea isn't enough. What makes it harder to copy is the real info a place builds up over time—like who shows up, who can do certain jobs, and which times are hardest to cover. Every place has a different mix of people and shifts; once staff get used to it and managers have their shift stuff in one spot, switching to something else is a hassle, especially if support is decent and people don't mind using it.

The reason it is not enough on its own is due to networking and history; it allows you to see projections of how many people switch shifts and on what days. This allows you to see if someone missed a shift or was in the wrong stations and on what day.

Anyone can throw up a basic shift website fast using AI, so the idea itself is easy to copy. What takes time is real store history, like which days have the most swaps, who misses shifts or ends up at the wrong station, and what actually works for that crew.

Once everyone gets used to checking it and managers keep all their shift info in one spot, switching to another app is a pain, especially if support is decent and people don't mind using it.

## 5. Cloud and Operations
Moving from a basic site to a real product takes logins, saved shift and skill info, payments, alerts, and privacy protection, plus managed cloud tools and backups so it doesn't crash or slow down as more stores join.

Moving from a basic design to a website that can actually run would include login info and keeping people's info private. Instead of dealing with lost data or website crashes. Using cloud tools and automatic backups makes it easier to keep things running smoothly.

A basic demo only needs simple hosting to show how pages look, while a live product has to handle real accounts, saved schedules and skills, payments, and shift alerts across stores. Logins keep each store's data separate, and a database saves schedules, claims, and skill info when lots of people use the site at once without losing updates.

As more stores join and traffic grows, managed cloud tools and automatic backups keep data safe without a small team managing raw servers on its own. Good support when login or scheduling issues pop up also helps prevent slowdowns and crashes.

## 6. Risks and Ethics
Since it has schedules and contact info, keeping people's info private and treating staff fairly matters—like not showing everyone's phone numbers, not playing favorites with shifts, letting workers keep personal reasons private, and not pressuring anyone into picking up extra shifts if they don't want to.

The app holds schedules and phone numbers; keeping people's info private matters. Coworkers shouldn't see everyone's personal phone numbers or have personal reasons for missing a shift shared with everyone, and picking up extra shifts should stay optional so nobody feels pressured into taking extra hours.

Keeping schedules and contact info locked to the right store and roles protects personal details from being shared around. Shifts should be handled fairly based on availability and skills rather than favoritism, so everyone gets a fair shot when hours open up.

Workers should be able to say no when they need rest, school time, or personal time off without feeling guilted into taking extra shifts. Keeping pickups optional helps the tool stay fair and helpful for the whole crew.
